## What each record contains

A record is a compact index entry for a mathematical result: statement, evidence path, verification method, and scope. The purpose is simple—when a result is cited later, the reader can see exactly what was established and where to inspect it.

## Corrections and failed checks

The ledger also keeps the cases where later checking changed the story:

- a fabricated benchmark citation generated during one workflow was detected and retracted;
- an Erdős 950 growth mechanism was refuted by its own numerical data;
- an attempted generalization made the resulting bound weaker rather than stronger and was retained as a negative result.

These records belong to the claims they correct. They are evidence that the ledger is revision-aware, not a summary of the other 49 mathematical entries.

## How to use the ledger

The ledger points to evidence; the underlying proof, computation, certificate, or formal file is the object to inspect. A status word in the ledger is not a substitute for that evidence.
