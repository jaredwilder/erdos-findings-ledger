# Erdős #1142 — exact subgroup residue sieve and certifier correction

**Status:** elementary necessary-condition theorem; historical novelty not claimed. A later internal strengthening is invalid as proved and is retracted from certificate authority.  
**Canonical problem:** OPEN.

Let `Good(n)` mean that `n-2^k` is prime for every integer `k>=1` with `2^k<n`.

## 1. Exact subgroup residue lemma

Let `p` be an odd prime and let

`d = ord_p(2)`.

If `Good(n)` and

`n > p + 2^d`,

then

`n mod p` is not in the multiplicative subgroup `<2>` generated by `2` in `F_p^*`.

Equivalently,

`n mod p in {0} union (F_p^* \ <2>)`.

### Proof

Assume instead that `n mod p` belongs to `<2>`. Then there is some exponent `j` with `1<=j<=d` such that

`n == 2^j (mod p)`.

Because `n>p+2^d>=p+2^j`, we have

`n-2^j > p > 1`.

But `p | n-2^j`, so `n-2^j` is composite. Also `2^j<n`, so this contradicts `Good(n)`.

Therefore the residue cannot lie in `<2>`.

## 2. Primitive-root corollary

If `2` is a primitive root modulo `p`, then `<2>=F_p^*`. Hence the only allowed residue is zero:

> If `Good(n)`, `2` is primitive mod `p`, and `n>p+2^(p-1)`, then `p|n`.

This primitive-root obstruction is classical: Uchiyama and Yorinaga (1976/77) proved the corresponding critical-prime proposition in their work on this Erdős problem. The present file records the slightly more general subgroup formulation explicitly; no priority claim is made.

## 3. Useful exact instances

The lemma gives immediately:

- `p=3`, `d=2`: for `n>7`, `3|n`;
- `p=5`, `d=4`: for `n>21`, `5|n`;
- `p=7`, `d=3`: for `n>15`,
  `n mod 7 in {0,3,5,6}`;
- `p=11`, `d=10`: for `n>1035`, `11|n`;
- `p=13`, `d=12`: for `n>4109`, `13|n`;
- `p=17`, `d=8`: for `n>273`,
  `n mod 17 in {0,3,5,6,7,10,11,12,14}`;
- `p=19`, `d=18`: for `n>262163`, `19|n`;
- `p=23`, `d=11`: for `n>2071`, the allowed residues are zero together with the complement of `<2>`.

For `n>21`, combining `3|n`, `5|n`, the `p=7` restriction, and the elementary fact that every Good `n>4` is odd yields

`n mod 105 in {0,45,75}`.

This is a genuine all-`n` necessary condition, not a finite-search extrapolation.

## 4. Internal certifier overclaim

A historical Pass-3 certifier later claimed:

> for `p in {3,5,7,11,13,17,19}`, the residues `2^k`, `1<=k<=ord_p(2)`, run over all nonzero residues modulo `p`;
> therefore every sufficiently large Good `n` is divisible by
> `3*5*7*11*13*17*19 = 4849845`.

The premise is false for at least `p=7` and `p=17`:

- `ord_7(2)=3`, whereas `|F_7^*|=6`; `<2>={1,2,4}`;
- `ord_17(2)=8`, whereas `|F_17^*|=16`; `<2>` has only eight nonzero residues.

Thus the certifier cannot promote `7|n` or `17|n` merely from this order argument. The claimed strengthened divisibility

`4849845 | n`

is therefore **not certified by the filed proof**, and every downstream count that assumes those two forced factors must be recomputed from a correct residue system.

This is an authority correction, not a counterexample to the divisibility conclusion itself: because no Good `n>105` is presently known, the conclusion may happen to hold vacuously or for another reason. What fails is the claimed proof/certificate.

## 5. Correct surviving large-threshold forced factors

From the primitive-root instances alone, any Good `n>262163` must be divisible by

`3*5*11*13*19 = 40755`.

In addition it must obey the nonzero-subgroup exclusions for primes such as `7` and `17`. Keeping those as residue restrictions is the sound form of the sieve.

## 6. Scope

None of these congruence filters resolves the two open questions:

- are there infinitely many Good `n`?;
- is there even one Good `n>105`?

They are necessary-condition compression only.

## Public state / literature

Erdős Problems #1142 remains OPEN and reports only the known values `4,7,15,21,45,75,105`, with historical finite exclusions and Vaughan's density bound. The primitive-root obstruction appears in the Uchiyama–Yorinaga line of work.
